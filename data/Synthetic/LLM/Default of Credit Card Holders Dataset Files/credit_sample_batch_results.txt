Key Findings:
- Default rate drifts to ~72/28 without constraints (real is 78/22)
  Claude overrepresents the minority class (defaults) without explicit targets
- LIMIT_BAL mean is CLOSER to real (174K vs real 167K) than constrained (146K)
  Sample rows helped the LLM learn realistic credit limits
- PAY_0 = 0 distribution is closer to real (49% vs real 49%) than constrained (43%)
- PAY>BILL violations are actually FEWER (2,458 vs 3,029)
  Sample rows may have helped Claude learn payment-bill relationships
- BILL>LIMIT violations dropped dramatically (30 vs 141)
  Sample rows clearly helped Claude learn bill-limit relationships
- Education distribution shifted — University overrepresented at 55% vs real 47%
- Batch-to-batch variance in default rate: 70-75% (wider than constrained)

Interesting tradeoff:
  Constrained prompt → better marginal distributions (default rate, education)
  Sample rows prompt → better logical consistency (fewer PAY>BILL, BILL>LIMIT violations)
  This is itself a finding about prompt engineering strategies.
